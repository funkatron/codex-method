# Initial Source Map

This is not a bibliography yet. It is a first map of sources and why they matter.

## Core mental models lineage

### Kenneth Craik, *The Nature of Explanation* (1943)

Often cited as an early source for the idea that organisms use small-scale models of external reality to anticipate outcomes before acting.

Why it matters: connects mental models to simulation, prediction, and action.

### P. N. Johnson-Laird, *Mental Models* (1983)

Foundational cognitive-science treatment of mental models in language, inference, and consciousness.

Why it matters: likely starting point for defining mental model in this project.

### P. N. Johnson-Laird, "Mental models and human reasoning" (2010)

Later synthesis of mental model theory and reasoning.

Why it matters: helps distinguish mature theory from the original book-length formulation.

### P. N. Johnson-Laird, "The history of mental models" (2005)

Historical account of the mental-model tradition.

Why it matters: identifies lineage and predecessor concepts; useful for avoiding accidental reinvention.

URL: https://modeltheory.org/papers/2005HistoryMentalModels.pdf

## Design / HCI lineage

### Don Norman, conceptual models and system image

Norman's design work distinguishes the designer's model, the user's model, and the system image through which the designer communicates with the user.

Why it matters: directly relevant to software, interfaces, and how external artifacts shape user mental models.

Starting secondary URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mental-models/

## Distributed cognition / external cognition

### Edwin Hutchins, "Distributed Cognition" (2000 encyclopedia entry)

Hutchins argues that cognition should not always be bounded by the individual skull; cognitive processes can be distributed across social groups, material artifacts, and time.

Why it matters: gives the project a rigorous frame for external representations as active components of cognition.

URL: https://arl.human.cornell.edu/linked%20docs/Hutchins_Distributed_Cognition.pdf

### Edwin Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild* (1995)

Major ethnographic study of cognition in real-world navigation systems.

Why it matters: likely a major source for understanding tools, maps, teams, and procedures as cognitive systems.

## ADHD-adjacent evidence

### Groen et al., "Testing the relation between ADHD and hyperfocus experiences" (2020)

Open-access study finding that hyperfocus experiences correlated with ADHD traits in a healthy adult sample but were not specific to adults with ADHD.

Why it matters: useful constraint against overclaiming that deep absorption or hyperfocus is unique to ADHD.

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0891422220302213

### Maisano et al., "ADHD symptom magnitude predicts creative problem-solving performance and insight versus analysis solving modes" (2026)

Reports association between higher ADHD symptom levels and relatively greater insight-based solving compared with analytic solving in a convergent-thinking task. Needs cautious treatment and replication.

Why it matters: possibly relevant to sudden comprehension, but does not establish an ADHD-specific model-formation pathway.

URL: https://gwern.net/doc/psychiatry/adhd/2026-maisano.pdf
