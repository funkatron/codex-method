# Codex Method Research Charter

## Purpose

The Codex Method investigates how deep understanding develops: how observations and information become organized into models that support explanation, prediction, transfer, revision, and communication.

The project begins from personal observations, but personal observation is not treated as proof. Observations are leads. Evidence determines what survives.

## Core question

How does a collection of information become a usable model of a complex system?

## Working commitments

These are commitments we are choosing consciously. They remain revisable.

1. Separate observation, evidence, hypothesis, speculation, and synthesis.
2. Treat models as tools, not identities.
3. Prefer questions that expose structure over questions that confirm intuition.
4. Make hidden commitments visible.
5. Optimize for cumulative understanding rather than volume.
6. Make knowledge accessible in license, format, modality, and explanation.
7. Revise when evidence requires revision.

## Non-goals

This project is not trying to:

- Diagnose a person.
- Prove that a particular cognitive style is superior.
- Claim that ADHD or autism uniquely explains the observed patterns.
- Produce academic-looking artifacts before the work requires them.
- Build a final taxonomy before the concepts have been investigated.

## Research posture

The preferred move is not:

> This supports our theory.

It is:

> This changes our current model.

## Accessibility principle

The project treats accessibility as part of understanding, not as a later publishing step.

A model that cannot be explained, explored, adapted, or represented in more than one modality may be incomplete for the purposes of this project.
