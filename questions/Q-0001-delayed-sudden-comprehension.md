# Q-0001: What mechanisms could produce delayed-but-sudden comprehension?

## Status

Initial research node. Low confidence. Evidence gathering has started.

## Prompting observation

The motivating personal observation is that comprehension sometimes appears only after an extended period of information collection. The subjective experience is not simply learning more facts, but a later transition in which the facts organize into a coherent explanatory model.

This observation should not be treated as evidence for a diagnosis-specific claim.

## Current question

What mechanisms could produce the subjective experience of delayed-but-sudden comprehension?

## Better version of the ADHD question

Instead of asking:

> Is this an ADHD thing?

ask:

> Does ADHD, or any related attentional/executive-function profile, alter the trajectory of mental model formation, insight, schema formation, or externalized cognition?

## Working distinction

```text
Observation:
  I often collect information for a long time before understanding appears to click.

Hypothesis:
  This pattern may be more common, more visible, or differently shaped in ADHD.

Current evidence status:
  Unknown. Do not state as conclusion.
```

## Candidate mechanisms

- Schema formation
- Chunking
- Conceptual change
- Insight / aha experience
- Mental model construction
- Memory consolidation
- Abstraction
- Cognitive compression
- External cognition
- Distributed cognition
- Interest-driven attention / hyperfocus
- Divergent and convergent problem solving

## Initial synthesis

The strongest early framing is not ADHD-specific. The broader phenomenon is delayed or sudden comprehension: the transition from accumulated information to organized, usable structure.

ADHD may still matter, but likely as a modifier of attention, motivation, information seeking, persistence, working memory, executive control, or insight style rather than as a standalone explanation.

## Early evidence notes

### Mental models

Johnson-Laird's mental model tradition frames reasoning and comprehension as involving internal models that can represent states of affairs and support inference. The historical lineage includes Kenneth Craik's idea of a small-scale model of external reality that allows alternatives to be tried out mentally before action.

Possible relevance: this supports the idea that understanding is not just possessing facts but having a model that can simulate or infer consequences.

### Distributed cognition

Hutchins' distributed cognition framework argues that cognitive processes may be distributed across people, artifacts, material environments, and time.

Possible relevance: diagrams, timelines, notes, maps, repositories, and tools may not merely record understanding; they may participate in the formation of understanding.

### ADHD / hyperfocus

Evidence on hyperfocus is mixed. One open-access study found that hyperfocus experiences are not specific to ADHD patients, although hyperfocus correlated positively with ADHD traits in a healthy adult sample.

Possible relevance: interest-driven deep engagement may support extended information collection, but this does not yet establish an ADHD-specific comprehension sequence.

### ADHD / insight

A recent study reports that higher ADHD symptom levels were associated with relatively greater use of insight over analytic solving in a convergent problem-solving task, but the authors call for replication and broader samples.

Possible relevance: this may connect ADHD traits to problem-solving style, but it should not be overgeneralized to mental model formation.

## Open questions

1. What is the difference between a schema, a mental model, and a conceptual model?
2. Does delayed comprehension correspond to schema formation, insight, conceptual change, or something else?
3. Are diagrams and other external representations part of the cognitive process, or just aids to it?
4. Does ADHD affect the timing, stability, or subjective experience of comprehension?
5. Are there existing terms for extended information collection before model formation?
6. How would this be tested without relying only on introspection?

## Potential predictions

If delayed-but-sudden comprehension reflects model formation, then after the transition a person should be better able to:

- Explain new cases
- Predict consequences
- Transfer knowledge to adjacent domains
- Identify exceptions and failure modes
- Compress many facts into fewer principles

If ADHD modifies this process, then differences may appear in:

- Information-seeking behavior
- Interest-driven persistence
- Switching costs
- Use of external representations
- Insight versus analytic problem-solving style
- Sensitivity to novelty, urgency, and challenge

## Research debt

- Need primary literature on schema theory.
- Need primary literature on insight learning.
- Need primary literature on conceptual change.
- Need stronger ADHD-specific sources beyond hyperfocus and creativity/insight.
- Need to avoid reifying popular coaching terms as scientific constructs.
